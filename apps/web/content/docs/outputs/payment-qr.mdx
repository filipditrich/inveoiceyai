---
title: Payment QR code
description: SPAYD QR platba so Czech banking apps pre-fill the payment.
---

Czech banking apps read **QR platba**, a QR code encoding a
[SPAYD](https://qr-platba.cz) payload. Your client scans the invoice, their app pre-fills account,
amount, variable symbol and due date, and they confirm.

No typing means no transposed account digits and no missing variable symbol — which is the actual
reason payments go missing.

## What is encoded

| SPAYD key | From                                                      |
| --------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| `ACC`     | Issuer IBAN, plus BIC when present                        |
| `AM`      | Total in koruna — major units, so `1210.50`, never haléře |
| `CC`      | `CZK`                                                     |
| `VS`      | Variable symbol                                           |
| `KS`      | Constant symbol, when set                                 |
| `SS`      | Specific symbol, when set                                 |
| `MSG`     | Invoice number, plus a shortened client name when it fits |
| `RN`      | Issuer name                                               |
| `DT`      | Due date, `YYYYMMDD`                                      |

Producing a payload like:

```text
SPD*1.0*ACC:CZ6508000000192000145399+GIBACZPX*AM:12100*CC:CZK*VS:20260007*...
```

Key order is fixed so the same invoice always produces the same code.

## When the QR appears

Only when all three hold:

- The payment method is **bank transfer**. Cash and card payments get no QR — there is nothing to
  transfer.
- The issuer has a **bank account** configured.
- The total is **positive**.

<Callout title="Credit notes get no QR">
  A dobropis has a negative total. Encoding a negative amount produces a code
  that either fails to scan or — worse — scans as a payment *to* your client.
  Invoicey omits it, and the refund is arranged the ordinary way.
</Callout>

## Rendering

The QR is generated as a PNG and embedded in the PDF next to the payment block, at high error
correction so it survives being printed, photocopied and scanned under office lighting.

## If it does not appear

Work down the list:

1. Payment method is not `transfer`.
2. The issuer has no IBAN — the account number alone is not enough, since SPAYD encodes IBAN.
3. The total is zero or negative.
4. The document is a credit note.

## Verify it once

Scan your first invoice with your own banking app before sending it to a client.

The IBAN and the printed account number are two separate fields on the issuer, and **nothing checks
that they describe the same account**. A mismatch means clients paying an account that is not yours —
and the QR is the field they will trust.
