## Rates

| Rate     | Czech                     | Typical use                                                                                                         |
| -------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **21 %** | základní sazba            | Most goods and services — IT, consulting, legal, electronics, fuel, telecom                                         |
| **12 %** | snížená sazba             | Accommodation, restaurant catering (not alcohol), passenger transport, residential construction, tap water, heating |
| **0 %**  | nulová sazba / osvobození | Books and periodicals, exports outside the EU, intra-EU supplies to VAT payers                                      |

The UI steers you toward 21 / 12 / 0, but any positive rate is accepted. This is intentional: a
backdated invoice with a 2022 DUZP may legitimately need 15 % or 10 %, which no longer exist as
current rates.

## VAT modes

`vat.mode` drives what appears on the PDF and how the ISDOC is tagged.

<Tabs items={['regular', 'reverse_charge', 'oss']}>

<Tab value="regular">

The standard domestic invoice. You charge VAT per line; the recipient deducts input VAT if eligible.

- Requires a DUZP.
- The PDF prints a full VAT recap (_Rekapitulace DPH_) grouped by rate.
- ISDOC emits standard `<TaxTotal>` blocks per rate.

If the issuer is **not** a VAT payer, this is still the mode used — every line is forced to 0 % and
the PDF prints _Nejsem plátce DPH_ where the breakdown would go.

</Tab>

<Tab value="reverse_charge">

**Přenesená daňová povinnost (PDP).** The recipient accounts for the VAT, not you. Mandated by §92
of the VAT Act for specific scenarios — construction and assembly work, scrap metal, mobile phones,
integrated circuits, and most cross-border B2B services within the EU.

What Invoicey does:

- Forces `vatRate = 0` on every line, and a VAT total of 0.
- Prints the legal note, defaulting to _"Daň odvede zákazník dle § 92a zákona č. 235/2004 Sb."_ —
  or the §92e variant for construction.
- Requires **both** parties to be VAT payers: the issuer must have `vatPayer = true` and the client
  must have a DIČ.
- Requires `vat.localReverseChargeCode`.

<Callout type="warn" title="localReverseChargeCode is not a paragraph number">
  This field takes a code from the PDP číselník — for example `4` for
  construction and assembly work — which lands in the ISDOC
  `LocalReverseChargeCode` element. Putting `92e` in it produces an ISDOC your
  accountant's software will reject. The §-reference belongs in the printed
  note, which Invoicey handles separately.
</Callout>

</Tab>

<Tab value="oss">

**One-Stop Shop.** The EU simplification for cross-border B2C: you charge the _destination
country's_ VAT rate and report it through a single return at home, instead of registering for VAT in
each country you sell into.

- The client is in another EU country and `vat.suppliesAbroad = 'eu'`.
- Line VAT rates are the destination country's, not Czech.
- The PDF states the destination rate and notes _Zdaněno dle pravidel OSS_.

OSS is in the schema and renders correctly, but the invoice builder keeps it behind an advanced
toggle rather than offering it by default — it is a mode most users should not reach for casually.

</Tab>

</Tabs>

## DUZP

**Datum uskutečnění zdanitelného plnění** — the date of taxable supply. It determines which tax
period the invoice belongs to, which is why it is a separate field from the issue date.

- **Required** on `invoice` and `credit_note`.
- May be earlier _or_ later than the issue date. Invoicing in March for work delivered in February
  means a February DUZP, and that is correct.
- On `proforma` and `advance` it may simply equal the issue date — neither is a tax document.

## Document types

| Type          | Czech                            | Tax document?                     |
| ------------- | -------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| `invoice`     | Faktura vystavená                | Yes                               |
| `proforma`    | Proformová faktura               | No — a request for payment        |
| `advance`     | Zálohová faktura                 | Yes, for the advance              |
| `credit_note` | Dobropis (opravný daňový doklad) | Yes — corrects a previous invoice |

A `credit_note` must reference the invoice it corrects via `meta.correctedInvoiceNumber`. The
schema enforces this.

Each type can have its own [numbering scheme](/docs/concepts/numbering) and counter.

## Not a VAT payer?

Set `vatPayer = false` on the issuer. Every line is forced to 0 %, the recap is replaced by
_Nejsem plátce DPH_, and the ISDOC reflects the same. Nothing else changes — you still get numbering,
QR codes, ISDOC and the full automation surface.
