---
title: Quickstart
description: From signing in to a sent invoice in about ten minutes.
---

This walks the whole path once: sign in, set up the business that issues invoices, add a client,
create an invoice, issue it, and send it. Everything after this page goes deeper on one step.

<Callout type="warn" title="You need a bank account and an IČO">
  Invoicey validates hard. It will not let you issue an invoice without a valid
  8-digit IČO, a Czech IBAN and a complete address — because an invoice missing
  those is not a valid Czech tax document. Have them at hand before you start.
</Callout>

<Steps>

<Step>

### Sign in

Invoicey uses OAuth only — **Google** or **GitHub**. There is no password to set and no
email-confirmation step.

Both providers verify email addresses, so signing in with Google and later with GitHub on the
same address links to one account rather than creating a second one.

First sign-in creates a personal [workspace](/docs/getting-started/workspaces) for you
automatically. All your data lives inside it.

</Step>

<Step>

### Create an issuer

An **issuer** is a business that issues invoices — you, or your s.r.o. Go to **Issuers → New**.

Type your **IČO** and Invoicey queries [ARES](/docs/guides/clients#ares-lookup), the Czech business
registry, filling in the legal name, address and DIČ. Check what came back; ARES is authoritative
for the registry, but it does not know your bank account or whether you want to be shown as a VAT
payer.

You must still supply:

- **Bank account** — Czech format (`19-2000145399/0800`) and the matching **IBAN**. This is what
  ends up in the [payment QR code](/docs/outputs/payment-qr).
- **VAT payer** — whether you charge DPH. If off, every line is forced to 0 % and the PDF prints
  _Nejsem plátce DPH_ instead of a VAT breakdown.
- **Contact email** — required. It becomes the `Reply-To` on invoice emails.
- **Numbering scheme** — how invoice numbers are generated. The default `{YYYY}{####}` produces
  `20260001`. See [numbering](/docs/concepts/numbering).

Optionally upload a **logo**, **stamp** and **signature** image; they are placed on the PDF.

</Step>

<Step>

### Add a client

**Clients → New**, then the same trick: enter the client's IČO and let ARES fill the rest. If you
do not know the IČO, search by company name instead.

For clients you will email invoices to, set **contact email** now. Without it, every send needs a
manually typed recipient.

</Step>

<Step>

### Create an invoice

**Invoices → New**. Pick the issuer and client you just made, then add line items — description,
quantity, unit price, VAT rate.

The important fields, all of which Invoicey pre-fills with sane defaults:

| Field                 | Meaning                                                                          |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Datum vystavení**   | Issue date                                                                       |
| **Datum splatnosti**  | Due date — must be on or after the issue date                                    |
| **DUZP**              | Date of taxable supply. Required on invoices and credit notes                    |
| **VAT mode**          | `regular`, `reverse charge` or `OSS` — see [Czech VAT](/docs/concepts/czech-vat) |
| **Variabilní symbol** | Payment reference. Defaults to the invoice number's digits                       |

Totals recompute as you type, per VAT rate, and are checked again server-side when you issue.

</Step>

<Step>

### Issue it

A saved invoice is a **draft**: no number, fully editable, not a tax document.

**Issue** turns it into a real one, in a single transaction: allocates the next number from your
scheme, freezes [snapshots](/docs/concepts/snapshots) of the issuer and client, recomputes totals,
renders the PDF and ISDOC, and stamps the issue date.

<Callout type="warn">
  Issuing is one-way. Issued invoices cannot be edited — only cancelled and
  re-issued. This is deliberate; see
  [statuses](/docs/concepts/statuses#why-issued-invoices-are-frozen).
</Callout>

</Step>

<Step>

### Send it

From the invoice detail, **Send email**. Invoicey attaches the PDF (always) and the ISDOC (on by
default) and sends via Resend.

The email comes from your deployment's verified sending domain with a display name of
`{Issuer name} via Invoicey`, and `Reply-To` set to the issuer's contact email — so replies reach
you, not the server. Delivery status (sent, delivered, bounced, opened) appears on the invoice
timeline.

</Step>

<Step>

### Mark it paid

When the money lands, **Mark paid**. Invoicey records the payment date; the status derives to
`paid` and it drops out of your overdue list.

Nothing marks invoices paid automatically — there is no bank integration.

</Step>

</Steps>
