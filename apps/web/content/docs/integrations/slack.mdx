Invoicey runs a durable agent in Slack. Mention it, or DM it, and describe the invoice you want — it
looks the client up in ARES and prepares a review card with the draft and its assumptions.
You decide when to issue, send or record payment.

## Using it

Mention the bot in a channel it has been invited to, or send it a direct message:

> @Invoicey prepare a draft using my workspace issuer for NFCtron (IČO 27074358),
> 40 hours of consulting at 1,800 CZK per hour, due in 14 days. Ask for missing dates,
> VAT and document language. Show the draft before issuing.

It replies in a thread with an interactive review card and a link into Invoicey. Draft revisions do
not automatically upload PDF and ISDOC files; use **Preview PDF** when you want a document preview.
The first time you talk to the bot, it DMs a link to confirm your Slack account against the Invoicey
workspace you are viewing in the browser.

**Follow-ups in the same thread do not need a mention.** The agent keeps session context, so you can
just say:

> Make it 45 hours and show me the updated draft. Do not issue it yet.

## Human approval on the dangerous parts

Review the displayed invoice before using an action button or explicitly approving a tool action:

| Tool                 | Why it asks                                               |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| `issue_invoice`      | Irreversible — allocates a number and freezes the invoice |
| `mark_invoice_paid`  | Asserts money arrived                                     |
| `send_invoice_email` | Reaches a real client's inbox                             |

Creating and revising drafts does not require approval.

<Callout type="warn" title="Link Slack before the bot will work">
  Unlinked Slack users are refused — no drafts, no ARES. The bot DMs a one-shot
  confirm link (15 minutes). Confirm it while viewing the Invoicey workspace
  that should receive **new** invoices. Custom invoice-card actions resolve the
  clicker's linked Invoicey workspace and re-check membership. A Slack approval
  prompt is not an invitation to the Invoicey workspace; keep review cards in
  channels appropriate for the invoice data.
</Callout>

The agent uses the same validated handlers as [MCP](/docs/integrations/mcp), plus Slack file upload.

## Getting the bot in your workspace

Ask an Invoicey workspace owner/admin to enable Slack under **[Workspace → Integrations](/settings/workspace/integrations)**, invite the
bot to the channel you will use, and complete the one-time Slack ↔ Invoicey link when the bot DMs
you.

## Limitations

- **Link required.** Each Slack user must confirm `/slack/link/[code]` against an Invoicey workspace
  they belong to. There is no email matching.
- **One Slack account → one Invoicey user + one workspace.** Rebind the workspace in
  **[Workspace → Integrations](/settings/workspace/integrations)**, or unlink so someone else can confirm.
- **Approval is not account linking.** Each Slack user links their own Invoicey identity. Keep invoice conversations within the intended team.
- **No slash commands.** Mentions and DMs only.
- **No import.** [Historical import](/docs/guides/importing-history) stays web-only.

## Troubleshooting

<Accordions>

<Accordion title="The bot never responds">
  Confirm it is invited to the channel, your Slack account is linked, and you
  are messaging from the workspace that completed the link. If it still stays
  quiet, ask a workspace admin to re-check **[Workspace →
  Integrations](/settings/workspace/integrations)**.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="The issuer is missing or incorrect">
  Open **My businesses** in the linked Invoicey workspace, complete the business
  and choose its default. The draft tool reads the seller server-side; it does
  not accept an issuer preset ID. If no usable issuer exists, drafting stops.
  Review the seller shown on the card before issuing. See [issuer
  setup](/docs/guides/issuers#presets-for-automation).
</Accordion>

</Accordions>
